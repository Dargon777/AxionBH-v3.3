AxionBH

An exploratory model connecting black-hole environments, plasma and axion-inspired mechanisms, electron–positron pair production, and positron transport to the observable Galactic 511-keV line. The project deliberately separates hypotheses, implemented calculations, and observational anchors instead of presenting one scenario as established physics.

What changed

From one strong idea to a testable chain of models

Early AxionBH versions were too categorical: they treated black holes as direct matter-to-antimatter converters and implied that this already explained baryon asymmetry and the 511-keV signal. In v8.x this was replaced by a layered architecture in which every link can be tested, disabled, or rejected independently.

corrected

511 keV: a rate, not erg/s

The Galactic quantity of order 10^43 refers to a positron annihilation/injection rate in e+/s, not an energy luminosity. The current model keeps these quantities separate and computes the 511-keV photon flux independently.

corrected

CVE ≠ CME

The axial chiral-vortical current and the magnetic chiral current are now treated as different physical objects. They are not automatically added together and do not become positron luminosity without additional microphysics.

implemented

Pair production is separated from the observed signal

A positron source is only the beginning. v8.8–v8.9 add source escape, slowing, spatial transport, annihilation, positronium branching, and conversion to an observable 511-keV line flux.

implemented

Plasma is now its own layer

The electron vector chemical potential can be supplied manually, inferred from density, or estimated through a reduced accretion closure. Finite-mass electron effects are handled separately from the old massless approximation.

Hypothesis map

What AxionBH actually assumes today

The status labels are not “true” or “false”. They indicate the modelling level: from speculative fundamental assumptions to standard astrophysical microphysics used as a diagnostic layer.

H1 · fundamental hypothesis

Curvature may activate a baryon-number-violating sector

The historical core of AxionBH remains a speculative idea: strong curvature and a pseudoscalar field may alter accessible CP/topological configurations. This is not treated as an established baryogenesis mechanism or as a proven simulator result.

H2 · phenomenological closure

Rotation and plasma may sustain chiral imbalance

The stationary branch uses an axial CVE source and a self-consistent closure for a chiral scale. Units and the quadratic structure have been audited, but the specific axion → μ5 → positron connection remains model-dependent and still needs a microscopic derivation.

H3 · kinematic audit

Scalar superradiance is a separate branch, not a universal source

For the scalar 211 state the model checks ωR < mΩH and estimates growth, saturation, and the required number of e-folds. The old spin-only threshold was removed. Superradiance by itself does not imply automatic MeV-positron production.

H4 · near-horizon source

A charge-starved gap may trigger a γγ pair cascade

v8.6–v8.7 implement the chain charge deficit → gap voltage → particle acceleration → curvature/IC photons → Breit–Wheeler pairs. Gap height is searched self-consistently; if the criteria are not met, the solver returns closure-not-found.

H5 · transport layer

The observed 511-keV morphology can depend on transport more than on the raw production rate

v8.8–v8.9 separate production, source escape, slowing, diffusion/advection or ballistic transport, bulge escape, and annihilation in different ISM phases. The same source can lead to very different observables under different transport regimes.

main open question

Does any channel actually close the full positron budget?

The workbench is no longer designed to tune the answer toward unity. It searches for bottlenecks: energy ceilings, plasma suppression, missing gap closure, insufficient escape/retention, or incompatible morphology. “Unreached” is a valid model result.

v8.9 architecture

From the horizon to the telescope

The most important change is the rejection of a single “efficiency” coefficient. The path to 511 keV is now split into physically distinct stages.

1. BH stateM, spin, ΩH, rg, geometry.
2. Accretionṁ, B, T, density and flow proxy.
3. PlasmaμV, finite-mass CVE, chirality diagnostics.
4. SourceCVE branch, superradiance or gap.
5. PairsSchwinger / γγ diagnostics and energy ceiling.
6. TransportEscape, slowing, diffusion/advection/ballistic.
7. 511 keVAnnihilation, positronium, Earth line flux.
Key audits

Where the model has already told itself to stop

These results matter more than a pretty match to the target because they reveal which old assumptions fail under stricter implementation.

5.2–9.5 × 10⁻⁹ M☉/yrPromising EHT GRMHD cluster for Sgr A*

The historical AxionBH value near 10⁻³ M☉/yr was more than five orders of magnitude higher. The current workbench therefore exposes accretion calibration instead of hiding it inside a preset.

≈0.961 × 10⁻³ ph cm⁻² s⁻¹Bulge 511-keV line-flux reference

The observed line is now compared as a photon flux and as a derived photon rate, rather than being mixed with an energy luminosity.

up to ~30 kpc / nH1-MeV field-aligned collisional path context

This makes magnetic topology and the transport regime critical: a long path along magnetic field lines does not imply the same spatial displacement from the source.

finite-mass plasma

The massless-electron approximation is no longer the baseline

At T = 10⁷ K the electron mass is still large relative to the thermal scale. v8.4.1 added a direct Fermi–Dirac integral and an explicit warning when massless transport formulas are used outside their reliable regime.

superradiance

The old 10⁻¹⁷ eV default fails the scalar-211 condition

For M = 4.28×10⁶ M☉ and a* = 0.89 the branch is no longer activated by a simple spin threshold. The physical superradiance condition is checked first.

transport uncertainty

One MeV positron, very different morphologies

In the v8.9 WNM example, diffusion+advection gives a characteristic smearing of about 12.8 pc, while a collisional/ballistic interpretation with a 1% field-line displacement gives about 4.7×10² pc. The difference is kept visible as structural uncertainty.

observable semantics

Production rate ≠ annihilation rate ≠ line-photon rate

Since v8.8 these quantities are separate. Positronium branching is explicit as well: para-Ps contributes two line photons, while ortho-Ps produces the three-photon continuum.

Current project philosophy

AxionBH is no longer “proof of a theory”; it is a machine for destroying bad assumptions.

A useful workbench result does not have to match the observation. If a branch breaks dimensional consistency, requires implausible accretion, cannot close a gap, exceeds an energy ceiling, or loses positrons during transport, that branch should be marked as problematic rather than rescued with a tuning coefficient.

Limitations

What cannot yet be treated as established physics

These are part of the model itself, not a small disclaimer.

No microscopic derivation of the full axion → chirality → e+ chain

Derivative axion couplings motivate axial backgrounds, but the specific stationary AxionBH closure remains a phenomenological ansatz.

The gap model is not GRPIC

The workbench does not solve the full Kerr Poisson problem, time-dependent feedback, a recursive cascade, or a self-consistent global magnetosphere.

ISM transport is reduced

It does not solve full 3D Galactic magnetic topology, detailed chemistry, dust, phase transitions, or a complete stopping-power network.

This is not a statistical fit

Explorer, sensitivity, inverse scans, and Missing Physics tools are deterministic diagnostics. They are not posteriors, confidence intervals, or observational detections.
